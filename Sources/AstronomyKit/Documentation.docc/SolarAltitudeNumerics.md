# Solar altitude: numerical accuracy

Derived partial arithmetic bounds and independent measurements for the native Sun altitude calculation.

## Scope and reference

``Sun/altitudeObservation(universalTime:from:deltaTModel:)``, ``Sun/altitudeObservation(terrestrialTime:from:deltaTModel:)`` and ``Sun/altitudeObservation(at:from:deltaTModel:)`` return airless solar altitude with the recorded UT, TT, observer and named Delta T model. Repeating `CelestialBody.sun.horizon(at: observation.time, from: observation.observer, refraction: .none)` returns the same altitude bits. The observation's ``SolarAltitudeObservation/ErrorBound`` is a partial numerical budget, not a bound on every numerical error or on the difference between the model and the sky. “Geometric altitude” here means without atmospheric refraction; the incoming direction is apparent.

The budget is derived for all three named Delta T models. ``DeltaTModel/espenakMeeus`` and ``DeltaTModel/jplHorizons`` use the existing polynomial slope, rounding, gap, step, and classification derivation. ``DeltaTModel/iersObserved`` uses separate bounds derived from every daily-table interval, each historical Hermite segment's endpoint and interior derivative extrema, and the analytic long-term length-of-day rate. Its source-derived magnitude and arithmetic guard also qualify the existing classification margin. The observed model is continuous at its regime joins, so it needs no step allowance.

The selected model combines the selected DE441 Earth barycenter plus geocenter offset minus its direct Sun source, the ICRS-to-EQJ bias and public frame round trip, the compiled DE441 barycentric Sun, elapsed-TDB light time, relativistic observer aberration, Vondrák long-term precession and pole-derived obliquity, IAU 2006/2000A nutation, all 33+1 complementary terms, and the IERS observer ellipsoid. The observer's reception position participates in light time, and its barycentric velocity includes the site's diurnal motion. The Sun does not deflect its own image. Shapiro delay, planetary deflection, physical ephemeris errors and refraction are outside this model.

The exact reference starts from the supplied scalar. A UT or TT `Double` is its exact binary64 value. A `Date` is its stored binary64 seconds since 2001-01-01; exact civil days are those seconds divided by 86400, plus 365.5. Before the civil table starts in 1961, civil days are the historical UT1 proxy; thereafter the exact table formula supplies TT. A TT input uses a mathematical inverse of its Delta T model. Where two UT solutions overlap, the recorded UT identifies the branch. A positive model gap has no reference UT and is refused. Emission uses TT/TDB directly and never evaluates a backdated Delta T polynomial.

Check modes reconstruct canonical coverage and compare source hashes; deleting a required record or changing a bound does not reduce the checked domain.

`reference.py`, `solar_reference.py` and `apparent_reference.py` evaluate the selected mathematical definitions at 80 decimal digits. Earth polynomials use the defining Chebyshev sum. Solar cubics use Hermite endpoint basis functions instead of native Chebyshev reconstruction. The reference independently solves the elapsed-TDB equation to a much smaller residual, applies relativistic aberration, and projects directly onto the local horizon. Published SOFA/ERFA checks and selected 80/110-digit comparisons qualify these implementations. Precision agreement at samples is not a continuous libm certificate. `generate_apparent_integration.py` separately records four independently differentiated observer trajectories.

Source hashes identify evaluated files; they do not establish mathematical validity. An expression change requires checking the derivation as well as recording new hashes. Runtime reports require fresh native captures after an owned input changes.

## Trajectory and observer bounds

The admitted reception hull lies in TT `[-36524.5,36889.5)`, entirely inside the selected Earth full-weight window. The derivation checks this containment and encloses every source record intersecting its clock-padded TDB image. Earth is the selected barycenter minus the direct Sun plus the geocenter offset. Binary64 coefficients are exact rationals. The identity `TᵢTⱼ=(Tᵢ₊ⱼ+T|ᵢ₋ⱼ|)/2` gives each component's squared-radius polynomial; its constant term plus or minus the sum of the remaining coefficient magnitudes encloses the complete record without sampling. Triangle bounds compose component radii. Derivatives use `|T′k| ≤ k²` and `|T″k| ≤ k²(k²−1)/3`, and TT acceleration includes both clock chain-rule terms. Integer-square-root enclosures bound vector norms. The ideal frame bias is orthogonal; a rational Gram-matrix bound accounts for the actual public EQJ-to-retained-frame-to-EQJ round trip.

The compiled solar source is cubic Hermite in TDB with shared position/velocity endpoints. Every record intersecting the padded budget domain is enclosed by coefficient majorants. Clock offset, first derivative and second derivative come from all 787 Fairhead terms and the JPL corrections. The TT speed bound multiplies the TDB speed by the clock-rate enclosure; acceleration includes both chain-rule terms. The source endpoints also cover the full accepted reception interval and private two-TT-day emission margin.

Site position is the ellipsoid vector rotated by GAST and inverse precession/nutation. Analytic first and second derivatives include Earth rotation, precession, nutation, obliquity and complementary terms. The trajectory derivative holds Delta T fixed, so `dUT/dTT=1`; coordinate beta divides the TT velocity by `dTDB/dTT` and the light speed in AU per TDB day. Pole square-root and cross-product lower bounds justify long-term normalization derivatives. The observer radius is bounded by `(6378.1366 + 10)/149597870.700` AU. The generated artifact records each position, velocity, acceleration and denominator enclosure.

## Time arithmetic and classification

Arithmetic arguments use binary64 round-to-nearest with `u=2^-53` and `γn=nu/(1−nu)`, without unsafe reassociation. Correctly rounded square roots and exact reduction modulo one are assumed where stated. Other interpolation, frame and math-library evaluation errors remain outside the partial budget.

The civil term covers epoch-offset addition, subtraction/division forming civil days, and the affine UTC-to-TT expression. The TT inverse term starts from the actual residual tolerance, includes forward-TT rounding, and divides by a positive lower slope. Across polynomial Delta T jumps, an absolute jump sum supplements smooth derivative bounds. Each named Delta T model remains a distinct input rather than an uncertainty estimate.

Classification uses separate outward majorants for the polynomial and observed Delta T evaluators over UT `[-37000,37000]`. The observed derivation binds the exact daily payload and native interpolation, Hermite, and long-term arithmetic graph. The common `1e-9`-day guard exceeds both derived forward-TT rounding bounds. This classification guard does not add Delta T evaluation error to the public altitude budget. Both reception UT values and their uncertainty are enclosed; one-sided jump allowances enclose polynomial-model TT images. The entire reception hull must lie inside the published budget domain and therefore inside the selected full-weight domain. Solar emission no longer widens that Earth hull because Earth is held at reception.

Positive-gap classifications use exact one-sided polynomials and outward binary64 endpoints. The observed model has no gaps in the accepted domain. Civil intervals that can intersect a polynomial gap or a UTC table segment start remain unsupported. Nonfinite inputs and invalid observers retain explicit errors. The observer budget is limited to 10 km above or below the ellipsoid.

## Solar light time and returned values

With reception TT `t`, fixed barycentric observer `O(t)`, solar trajectory `S`, and clock offset `d(t)=TDB(t)−t` in days, the delay map is `F(τ)=|S(t−τ)−O(t)|/c−d(t)+d(t−τ)`. The certificate proves that it maps `[0,0.006]` into itself and has Lipschitz constant `k=Vsun/c+max|TDB′−1|<1`. Shared solar endpoints make the source C1, and emission has no Delta T discontinuity. The historical exact-real backdating cycles therefore do not belong to this composition.

Exact-model successive differences are bounded by `T*k^(n−1)`. They fall below the minimum native four-delay-ULP threshold before the 20-evaluation cap. Native stopping accepts the larger of four delay ULP and one emission-TT ULP, or a repeated rounded emission TT. The emission-lattice term prevents a neighboring-epoch cycle when the delay itself has much finer spacing. The certificate bounds the arithmetic residual implied by either successful stop and divides it by `1−k` to bound exact-root displacement, conditional on separately excluded interpolation, norm and clock-evaluation errors. It does not prove that every binary64 input returns successfully: the native loop is bounded and may throw `noConvergence`. Exact-model contraction and sampled successful native calls are distinct evidence.

The light-time term propagates root displacement through solar speed and the angular correction. It also includes the sum of one possible reception position/velocity join in each selected Earth source component; velocity joins affect aberration. The uncertainty hull is shorter than one TT day, while consecutive joins of any component are more than 3.999999 TT days apart. Exact endpoint sums enclose every join in the padded domain, including simultaneous joins. For longer interval enclosures, selected seams lie on `TDB=-36560.5+4*k`; the barycenter and direct Sun have joins at `TDB=-36568.5+16*k`. The retained public eight-TT-day spacing is compatibility metadata and does not enumerate these seams. Count the crossed TDB grid points, or conservatively apply `floor(TT interval length / 3.999999)+1` summed join allowances. No emission Delta T step supplement remains. The public field retains the name `lightTimeTermination` for compatibility and documents this join allowance.

## Altitude sensitivities and exclusions

The relativistic map is normalized from the ERFA `eraAb` expression. Rational beta and solar-distance bounds give a positive unnormalized norm. Its direction Lipschitz factor is bounded by 2, its beta factor by 4, and its distance derivative separately. These bounds compose observer position/acceleration, solar retarded motion, TT/TDB conversion, and horizon rotation. ERA rounding affects both horizon direction and the observer position/velocity used in the apparent-place solve; all these paths contribute to its term.

UT and TT sensitivities are bounded separately. The TT bound conservatively includes complete site motion, even where holding UT fixed would remove its dominant rotation. Earth join bounds include position and velocity differences. The denominator margin is checked against all composed time perturbations. `derived-altitude.json` retains the exact rational expressions and outward constants rather than fitting them to observed maxima. ``SolarAltitudeObservation/ErrorBound/total`` rounds additions outward.

The partial budget excludes interpolation evaluation, TT/TDB series evaluation, relativistic-map arithmetic, precession/nutation and sidereal evaluation, horizon projection, libm, Delta T evaluation, source interpolation accuracy, and physical model error. Their propagation is also excluded. The observed model's published uncertainty is about one standard error rather than a proved bound. ``SolarAltitudeObservation/statedUncertainty`` reports it separately with its propagated altitude estimate; ``SolarAltitudeObservation/ErrorBound/total`` never includes it. A measured residual below the partial sum does not establish that excluded errors vanish.

Each full-altitude configuration records 1,908 canonical requests across all three models and six observers, including poles, heights of ±10 km, coverage/fallback epochs, adjacent source/model seams, the observed table and prediction joins, historical backdating neighborhoods, and civil inputs. Reports retain native bits, actual barycentric solar callbacks, extended-precision root traces, public budgets or unsupported reasons, the observed model's separate stated uncertainty, exact-input references, source hashes, compiler identity, and process measurements. Primitive reports retain their separate 162-row grids.

The captured Debug and Release results and their maxima are in `Scripts/solar-numerics/evidence`; reproduction commands are in its parent README. Paired-altitude identity is asserted within a configuration, not promised across configurations. These finite grids do not prove continuous model or libm accuracy. Process RSS and latency are measurement-process observations, not library-only retained-memory bounds. Independent Horizons comparisons retain their separately documented source and limits.
