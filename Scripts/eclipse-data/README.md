# Native eclipse evidence

The records below provide sampled qualifications for native lunar eclipses, global and local solar eclipses, and Mercury/Venus transits across the four issue #93 links. Each section records its source conventions, measured scope and remaining gaps. The public eclipse and transit facades run on these searches since the #96 cutover; the final acceptance matrix preserves the outstanding #93 qualifications.

`native-captures.json` records Debug and Release results for six NASA lunar eclipses, a fresh-process Release workload of one cold search followed by 99 next-event searches, build receipts, object sizes, the test executable size, the tested base revision and the toolchain. `native-evidence.json` checks the existing peak and duration allowances, independently derives residuals from each captured native peak and source time, verifies Debug/Release agreement, and binds the resource observations to their exact call counts. The resource values describe one host and establish no portable ceiling.

The selected NASA catalog cases cover 1800, 2000, 2001 and 2099. The pinned NASA Observer's Handbook 2001 page supplies exact UT peaks for a total, partial and penumbral eclipse. Their phase durations come from the modern Five Millennium Catalog, which identifies Danjon's shadow-enlargement method and publishes its greatest-eclipse coordinate in TD; the fixture does not reinterpret that TD coordinate as UT. NASA lunar eclipse magnitude is a fraction of the Moon's diameter immersed in a shadow. It is retained with that meaning and is not relabeled as disc-area obscuration.

NASA SVS pages 4953 and 5672 directly publish 99.1% and 96.3% Moon-disc obscuration at greatest eclipse in 2021 and 2026. Assuming the printed percentages were rounded to the nearest 0.1 percentage point, the inclusive comparison intervals add and subtract 0.0005 from the printed fractions; inclusive endpoints avoid assuming a tie rule. Both pages come from one publisher and the same disclosed DE421/LOLA model family, and neither page publishes its exact rounding rule, lunar and shadow radii, or full-precision area algorithm. The intervals are inferred print intervals rather than NASA uncertainty bounds or an engine tolerance. The native geometry evaluates NASA's published Danjon angular shadow equations on a tangent plane at the Moon's geocentric distance. Agreement inside these narrow intervals shows consistency with the printed values under the stated rounding assumption; it does not establish exact replication of the SVS algorithm. After #256 apparent-place integration, the 2026 result is 0.9624960076722118, below the unchanged [0.9625,0.9635] interval. That assertion remains a narrowly tracked expected failure under #275; the 2021 gate remains ordinary. The exact physical/source cause is unresolved. An independent 80-digit overlap using archived Horizons apparent angles and astrometric ranges also falls below the interval, and the alternative angular-separation peak does not restore agreement. Production shadow/projection/overlap arithmetic is separately gated against the raw-input oracle at 1e-12 fraction, which is not a physical uncertainty allowance.

The bounded [SVS source replay](../svs-lunar-obscuration/README.md) now also interpolates the disclosed DE421 kernel directly. DE421 and DE441 simultaneous geometric calculations differ by only `1.54e-8` in obscuration at the retained peak, while the archived apparent-versus-geometric DE441 calculations differ by about `0.0008997`. The published peak candidates and four source-defined circular shadow formulas do not reproduce the conditional print interval as a complete convention. SVS does not disclose whether its area calculation used apparent or geometric directions, its full peak, radii, LOLA limb treatment, projection, overlap algorithm, full-precision value, or rounding, so the investigation does not establish a runtime defect or close #275.

## Native global solar eclipses

The second #93 link adds the internal global search. `global-references.json` is rebuilt by `Scripts/capture-global-solar.py` from the pinned NASA catalog and RP1301 sources under `Scripts/reference-data/sources/global-solar/`. Every new publisher response has an adjacent query recipe; the capture tool downloads and validates the complete candidate set before publication. The archive and recorder stage complete file sets, replace individual files atomically and roll back earlier replacements after an ordinary publication error. This does not promise cross-file crash atomicity; a rollback failure retains backups and reports their location. `global-captures.json` and `global-evidence.json` record Debug/Release outputs, source-derived timing/location/area comparisons and host costs. The recorder checks input identities, TT/UT relations, partial nil/NaN representation, finite central fields, angular-radius-to-area relations, source allowances and cross-configuration agreement. All directly used engine, public input, fixture, test, parser and recorder sources are bound by digest.

The original three global events retain 453.6 seconds and 0.247 degrees. Four additional catalog cases cover 2014 non-central annular, 2043 non-central total, 2023 hybrid with total geometry at greatest eclipse and 2025 partial. A hybrid is a path classification, not a promise of totality at its peak. The finite umbral/antumbral cone can intersect Earth when its axis misses the geoid; the public C shortcut that misclassifies both non-central events remains tracked in [#96](https://github.com/heirloomlogic/AstronomyKit/issues/96#issuecomment-6079064119).

Solar-only geometry uses the historical 959.63 arcsecond Astronomical Ephemeris semidiameter at one au, published in [NASA/JPL's constants report, Table 9](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19690001472/downloads/19690001472.pdf), converted with the sine of that angle and the engine's au of 149,597,870.700 km. The independent discriminator uses that same engine conversion; this exact au value is not attributed retrospectively to RP1301. The umbral lunar radius is NASA's k2=0.272281 times the Earth equatorial radius; penumbral discovery uses the modern catalog's k1=0.272488. These are scoped solar-model choices. The qualified lunar-shadow constants remain unchanged. Neither the modern IAU nominal solar radius nor C's 14-meter classification bias establishes RP1301's convention.

RP1301 uses DE200/LE200, k1=0.2725076 and k2=0.272281. Its Table 4 directly publishes annular obscuration 0.8895 at 17:10 and 17:15 UT on 1994-05-10. Both native area results fall in [0.88945,0.88955], conditional on nearest-four-decimal printing; the publisher supplies no rounding rule or uncertainty bound. The source TT is the printed UT plus the report's fixed 59.5-second Delta T. Geometry is anchored at that TT with a model-consistent Espenak-Meeus time, so subsequent light-time steps retain the model. Captures keep the published `sourceUT` separate from `modelUT`, and the recorder verifies both conversions. Combining the fixed source pair with a different model would shift derived TT by about 0.763 seconds; the regression checks zero-day and light-time derivations.

RP1301 Table 1 labels 0.94314 as `Eclipse Magnitude`; only Table 4 labels its rounded 0.9431 column `Diameter Ratio`. `Scripts/assess-rp1301-g0.py` evaluates the published Besselian polynomials at 17:12:26.49 TDT, intersects the shadow axis with the 0.99664719 polar-to-equatorial ellipsoid in the [archived NASA JavaScript Solar Eclipse Explorer source](../reference-data/sources/global-solar/nasa-jsex-program.js), reduces each cone radius by `L'=l-zeta*tan(f)`, and applies its central magnitude relation `(L1'-L2')/(L1'+L2')`. The archived program has SHA-256 `9676f7922ced83c47fc088af5b8f53f7f51cc13563e527ee53910c54ed61c881` and retains its GNU GPL version 2-or-later header; the [accompanying GPLv2 text](../reference-data/sources/global-solar/COPYING.GPL-2.0) and both query receipts are digest-bound. Those GPL terms apply to the archived third-party JSEX program, not AstronomyKit's separately licensed code. The result, 0.9431418141, lies inside the conditional [0.943135,0.943145] print interval and reproduces G0 without fitting a constant. RP1301 constructs l1 with k1=0.2725076 and l2 with k2=0.2722810, so this result mixes two lunar-radius conventions. Its square, 0.8895164815, is not a physical single-radius overlap area. The four physical candidates at G0 range from 0.8894666294 to 0.8902134473; the retained historical-solar-radius plus k2 case is 0.8894736712. `rp1301-g0-decomposition.json` preserves the complete calculation beside the two direct Table 4 controls. The report says its algorithms derive primarily from the 1974 Explanatory Supplement, but that edition is not in the replay archive; the evidence establishes agreement with the published RP1301 elements and the archived 2007 NASA implementation, not a page-level audit of the unavailable book.

The archive parser extracts the unique 1986-10-03 TD catalog row and the date-specific beaded-annular statement, rejecting contradictory content even after a digest refresh. The evidence derives its source metadata and comparison epoch from those parsed fields. This boundary discriminator is diagnostic: the source catalog prints H and magnitude 1.0000, while RP1301's lunar-limb discussion describes a beaded-annular observation. At the printed TD epoch, k2 plus the historical solar semidiameter produces a small positive mean-limb umbra and a ratio about 1.0000164, hence total under this smooth-disc model. The retained C radii and its 14-meter bias do not resolve the limb discrepancy. Rounded 1.0000 alone cannot supply an exact peak-kind oracle. No lunar limb profile or observed beading is modeled here.

`radius-vectors.json` and `radius-comparison.json` retain the bounded four-encoding discriminator at the two Table 4 epochs, G0 and the 1986 source epoch. `Scripts/global-solar-radius-probe.swift` emits the native vectors; `Scripts/assess-global-solar-radii.py` independently computes central surface angles and the radius comparison. `diameterRatioSquared` can exceed one in a total case; `areaObscuration` is capped at one by full containment, not by C's annular cap. The reported signed umbra is the straight-cone diagnostic, not an asserted lunar-limb observable.

## Local solar circumstances

`local-references.json`, `local-captures.json` and `local-evidence.json` qualify the native local search and next-event path. The existing three EclipseWise cases retain their 60-second contact and 0.5-degree altitude allowances. Their archive bytes and legacy `UTC` field names are unchanged, but the source labels require UT1 calendar decoding. The original [April map](https://www.eclipsewise.com/solar/SEgmapx/2001-2100/SE2024Apr08Tgmapx.html) identifies DE430 and Delta T 69.2 s; the original [October map](https://www.eclipsewise.com/solar/SEgmapx/2001-2100/SE2024Oct02Agmapx.html) identifies DE430 and 70.7 s. Both explicitly publish UT1, including the default map time-zone label. The separate older April `SEcirc` calculator uses DE406 and 71.3 s and is not substituted for these maps. Primary browser text was readable when direct HTTP requests returned 403; the original pinned numerical table remains replayable, while these map-page semantic statements are linked rather than represented as a captured raw response. The corrected public audit and native tests compare UT coordinates, preserving the caller's model for evaluated geometry. The evidence retains each former UTC residual beside the corrected UT1 residual; this is a source interpretation correction, not an engine accuracy gain.

NASA RP1301 [Table 8](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEpubs/19940510/tables/table.8) and [Table 10](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEpubs/19940510/tables/table.10) directly publish local contacts, maximum, solar altitude and obscuration for Acapulco and Springfield, Illinois. Coordinates and elevations are parsed from the selected rows, including the state disambiguation for Springfield. These source contacts are UT under DE200/LE200 and fixed Delta T 59.5 s. The source-epoch area comparison first forms TT = source UT + 59.5 s and constructs a model-consistent engine time at that TT; source UT and model UT remain separate. Two printed local areas, 0.421 and 0.889, pass inclusive nearest-0.001 print intervals. This conditional rounding assumption is not a publisher uncertainty or a new engine tolerance. Search contact/altitude checks retain 60 s/0.5°. They do not resolve the finer global G0 ratio-derived discrepancy or the 1986 limb qualification.

The static [NASA Washington catalog](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEcirc/SEcircNA/WashingtonDC1%2B21.html) lists every visible solar eclipse at 38°53.0′ N, 77°02.0′ W. The adjacent 2026-08-12 and 2028-01-26 rows independently qualify native next-event identity; both are locally partial and above the horizon. Published local standard clocks use GMT−5, with no daylight-saving adjustment. The [key](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEcirc/SEcirckey.html) and [historical model](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEcirc/SEpredictions.html) are archived alongside the table. Its Newcomb/Brown ephemerides, extrapolated Delta T and minute precision do not establish a modern contact-time or area tolerance. Tests compare calendar identities and use zero elevation explicitly because the source omits elevation. They do not relabel GMT as an exact UTC or UT1 observation.

## Transits, penumbral contacts and final acceptance

`transit-references.json`, `transit-captures.json` and `transit-evidence.json` record the fourth and final native link. NASA's [Mercury](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/transit/catalog/MercuryCatalog.html) and [Venus](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/transit/catalog/VenusCatalog.html) catalogs define geocentric Universal Time contacts, greatest transit as closest centers from Earth's center, and separation in arcseconds. The fixture converts separation to arcminutes. The catalog does not document exact physical radii, limb shape, aberration/light-time settings or a numerical minimizer. Its Besselian elements are attributed to Meeus, *Transits* (1989); later individual NASA event pages are not retroactively used as the provenance for every catalog row.

The engine minimizes angular center separation and finds external angular-disc tangencies using native apparent geocentric vectors. The [Mercury](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/mercuryfact.html), [Venus](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/venusfact.html) and [Sun](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/sunfact.html) fact sheets independently support the selected spherical mean radii. Their pages and query recipes are pinned. These conventions are not inferred from agreement with the transit table or attributed to its undocumented implementation. No solar-eclipse 14-meter bias or rise/set optical radius is used. Contact order, sign changes around roots, minimum-separation neighbors, captured models, canonical identity, exact cutoff neighbors, repeated clamps, long Venus next-event gaps, invalid bodies and accepted endpoints are tested.

Six existing transit samples preserve Mercury's 642.6-second/0.2121-arcminute and Venus's 546.54-second/0.6772-arcminute allowances. The legacy Z-shaped fixture fields are UT calendar coordinates, not UTC instants. Correcting the public audit's decoding changes its source residuals without changing public C calculations or any tolerance. Both former and corrected public peak residuals are recorded. The former future-UTC diagnostic uses the library’s current civil-offset table; it does not predict future leap seconds. Complete catalog adjacency independently establishes Mercury 2003→2006 and Venus 2012→2117→2125. The eclipse catalogs similarly establish lunar January→July→December 2001 and global April→October 2024→March 2025; the local Washington sequence remains in the local evidence owner.

The [January](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEplot/LEplot2001/LE2001Jan09T.pdf), [July](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEplot/LEplot2001/LE2001Jul05P.pdf) and [December](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEplot/LEplot2001/LE2001Dec30N.pdf) 2001 NASA diagrams publish actual P1/P4 contacts in UT, `CdT (Danjon)`, VSOP87/ELP2000-85, and rounded Delta T 64 seconds. Their printed angular shadow radii and Sun/Moon semidiameters are retained as source metadata, not substituted into native geometry. The PDFs use outlined text, so the adjacent transcription explicitly records manual visual extraction and is digest-bound to the reviewed files. Regeneration verifies those pinned bytes and recipes; it does not pretend to re-read outlined glyphs. Source UT contacts are compared with native model UT, without constructing an inconsistent TT/UT pair from the diagram's rounded Delta T.

All six diagram P1/P4 comparisons meet the unchanged 120-second allowance. The original [OH2001](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/OH/OH2001.html) comparison remains separate: its Newcomb/Improved Lunar Ephemeris and 2% shadow enlargement produce December ingress/egress residuals of about +131.878/−122.901 seconds, outside that allowance. January and July remain inside. The matching-model qualification does not turn the old December comparisons into passes, prove that shadow enlargement alone explains the residual, or establish equivalence between historical conventions. Actual ingress/egress roots now share the same helper used to calculate native lunar semidurations; no physical lunar model was changed.
