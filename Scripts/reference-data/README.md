# Reference data

Published truth data for the shipping test suite: archived JPL Horizons responses, USNO and NASA catalog tables, and the generator that turns them into offline test fixtures. Tests never contact a network service.

The catalog tables are Astronomy Engine's transformations of USNO season, moon-phase and rise/set output, Fred Espenak's AstroPixels node table, NASA GSFC eclipse and transit catalogs, and the EclipseWise local solar eclipse table, pinned to revision `865d3da7d8112bbc7911238052c6af4aaf877181`. `sources/astronomy-engine-license.txt` is the license for those copied files. `sources/geo_moon_states.txt` and `sources/geo_emb_states.txt` are Astronomy Engine's copies of JPL Horizons (DE441) geometric states of the Moon and the Earth-Moon barycenter; they are NASA/JPL output, so they are used under the same acknowledgment and no-endorsement terms as the Horizons responses, and the MIT license applies to the copies. `sources/mooninfo_2020.txt` to `mooninfo_2022.txt` are Astronomy Engine's copies of NASA Scientific Visualization Studio's Moon Phase and Libration tables (svs.gsfc.nasa.gov 4768, 4874 and 4955); NASA factual data, used with acknowledgment and without implied endorsement, and the MIT license applies to the copies. `EngineLibrationTests` reads them directly; `build-fixtures.py --check` verifies their SHA-256 but builds no fixture from them. `sources/naif/pck00011.tpc` is NAIF's planetary constants kernel (NASA/JPL, 2022-12-27), copied unchanged; NASA/JPL factual data, used with acknowledgment and without implied endorsement. `EngineRotationAxisTests` reads it directly, and `build-fixtures.py` pins its URL and SHA-256. Each Horizons response sits beside a `.query.json` recipe holding every API parameter and the response's SHA-256. `sources/jpl-validation` holds, in the same form, the Horizons observer tables that the geocentric Uranus and Neptune rows of `JPLValidationTests` match, which `RecordedHorizonsTableTests` checks; `build-fixtures.py --check` verifies each response against its recipe's SHA-256 but builds no fixture from them, and the other `JPLValidationTests` rows have no recorded query.

The lunar phase (90 s), lunar eclipse (2 min) and rise/set (1.18 min) tolerances, and the relative limits on the Moon and Earth-Moon barycenter states, are constants in `build-fixtures.py`, taken from Astronomy Engine's C test harness (`generate/ctest.c`) at the same revision. The 2 s allowance on the four January 2100 Horizons phase roots (`apparentLunarPhases`) does not come from `ctest.c`: the owner set it for those rows on #247, against native residuals of 0.657 to 0.819 s.

`sources/sofa/sofa_ast_example.c` is the IAU SOFA 2023-10-11 astrometry example, copied unchanged and pinned by its publisher URL and SHA-256. It supplies the star's ICRS position, proper motion, parallax, site and instant. `sofa-fixed-star-reference.c` runs the same transformations with proper motion set to zero because `FixedStar` stores no proper-motion terms, and produces the values archived in `sources/sofa/fixed-star-reference.json`. The JSON pins the recipe hash and the official `sofa_c-20231011.tar.gz` archive hash (`d9c10833cae8b4d9361a0ffda31ec361fd1262362025bec4d4e51a880150ace2`). Every fixture build extracts the pinned archive, compiles the recipe against the required SOFA sources, runs it, and compares every emitted value with the archived row. The 0.5 arcsecond test limit is twice the largest 0.246 arcsecond residual sampled from this row, rounded up; it is a regression margin around SOFA's definitions, not a published or certified accuracy.

`sources/constellations` pins the official IAU page of 88 constellation names and abbreviations and CDS/VizieR catalog VI/42's ReadMe, 357-row B1875 boundary table and C program, each by publisher URL and SHA-256. VI/42 is Nancy Grace Roman's 1987 rearrangement of Delporte's 1930 IAU boundaries; its ReadMe records a correction to row 229 on 1999-12-30. Every fixture build compiles the unchanged C program and runs all eight ReadMe examples against the pinned boundary file. The Python generator separately translates the program's Herget mean-place precession, emits one J2000 sample near every segment and three exact B1875 ties per segment, and generates `Sources/AstronomyKit/Engine/Stars/Generated/ConstellationData.swift`. `--check` rejects a changed source hash, fixture archive or Swift table. The `< 1e-9` checks on generated J2000 samples measure agreement between the Python and Swift translations at those points; the exact tie checks apply the table's inclusive lower and exclusive upper bounds and carry no angular-accuracy claim.

Each `allowedErrorKm` in `distance-fixtures.json` is 2 × max(characterization maximum, literature floor) + 0.001 km + alignment allowance, rounded up to a multiple of 0.001 km. The characterization maximum is the largest range error AstronomyKit showed for that body and mode against a separate set of Horizons vectors on epochs disjoint from `sources/distance/heldout`. The literature floor is the published ephemeris accuracy scale for the body in km (Mercury 0.347, Venus 2.705, Earth 3.740, Mars 22.794, Jupiter 272.404, Saturn 1000.554, Uranus 229.999, Neptune 1891.819; the Sun uses Earth's, the Moon and Pluto use 0), plus Earth's 3.740 for geocentric series other than the Sun and Moon. The alignment allowance is 1 km for geocentric series other than the Moon and 0 otherwise.

## Coverage

One row per public calculation family: the published source, the test that checks it, the tolerance, the dates it covers, and the kind of evidence. A family with no published check is marked gap and names the open issue that owns the missing data. Evidence kinds:

- Published-value check: compares AstronomyKit with a number or definition from a cited external source.
- Mathematical bound: a derived bound or identity, or agreement with another path inside AstronomyKit. No external value.
- Named sanity check: plausibility only (finite, in range, ordered, or a tolerance too wide to bound accuracy). Not accuracy evidence.

Test names are in `Tests/AstronomyKitTests`. Tolerances marked `ctest.c` are limits in Astronomy Engine's C test harness at revision `865d3da7d8112bbc7911238052c6af4aaf877181`.

The relative-longitude fixture does not use apparent geocentric longitude. `build-fixtures.py` pairs Earth and planet vectors at each JDTDB, requests `VEC_CORR=NONE` from the Sun center, and runs the pinned official SOFA IAU 1976/1980 precession-nutation routines through `sofa-angular-events.c` before evaluating `direction × (Earth longitude − planet longitude)`. Direction is +1 for Mars and −1 for Venus, matching `Astronomy_SearchRelativeLongitude`. Each retained row straddles one forward root, including the two zero-degree wrap crossings.

The maximum-elongation fixture uses Horizons quantity 23, not ecliptic separation. Horizons defines S-O-T as the apparent full-sky angle at the observer and `/T` or `/L` as the target trailing or leading the Sun. The archived one-hour samples bracket the printed maximum and retain all equal-height samples when four-decimal output forms a plateau. The public search finds a zero of a centered 0.1-day finite-difference slope, so its numerical definition remains separate from the source sampling resolution.

Not counted as evidence anywhere above: recorded engine output in `ReproducibilityTests`, the legacy Moon and Pluto values in `BundledEphemerisTests`, the recorded values in `DeltaTModelCaptureTests` and the golden bits in `InverseRefractionTests`.
