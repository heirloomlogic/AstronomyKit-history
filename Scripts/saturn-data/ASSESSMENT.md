# Saturn body-center trajectory and planetary apsis evidence

Issue #92 requires the API's physical planet center relative to the Sun center. The six archived Saturn references use Horizons target 699 (reported source `sat441l`), center `500@10`, geometric ICRF vectors, AU-D and explicit JD TT. The additional `barycenter-reference.json` repeats those epochs with target 6; its query and response digest are adjacent. At JD TT 2463565.54855741, the center range rate is 3.51454e-12 AU/day while the Saturn-system barycenter's is 8.02915e-8 AU/day. The 299.4 km center offset changes the nearly stationary radial curve enough to move its apsis. The old native rate was 7.72964e-8 AU/day, close to the barycenter but still different enough that adding only the satellite offset would not establish sixty-second accuracy.

## Source and representation

`assess-saturn-apsides.py` uses the existing strict DAF/type-2 reader and verified DE441 cache. It retrieves SAT441 from [NAIF](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/satellites/sat441.bsp), selects target 699 relative to 6, and combines it with DE441 targets 6 and 10 relative to the solar-system barycenter. The physical heliocentric state is `(6 − 10) + (699 − 6)` on ICRF axes. [JPL's satellite ephemeris listing](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/ephem/files.html) and [SAT441 comments](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/satellites/sat441.cmt) describe the satellite source. The assessment pins HTTP identity, every retrieved byte range, source segment descriptors, record selection and all encoded payload digests. The cached receipts must agree with both the source identity and the pinned range digest.

The selected window contains 56,292 SAT441 records, 2,640 DE441 Saturn-barycenter records and 5,278 DE441 Sun records. All Chebyshev coefficients are retained. The direct Float64 candidate uses 19,400,016 bytes. Keeping the two large barycentric terms in Float64 and the small satellite offset in Float32 uses 10,618,464 bytes. The latter is the integrated candidate. Full-record coefficient-difference bounds enclose Float32 representation error by 1.9733917163714793e-5 km in position and 7.303156184805486e-6 km per TDB day in analytic velocity, before evaluator roundoff. They use `|T_n| ≤ 1` and `|T'_n| ≤ n²` with outward-rounded arithmetic. These are encoding bounds relative to the source, not claims about SAT441's physical accuracy. The assessment also evaluates every adjacent record pair in both encodings. Float32 offset records have maximum endpoint jumps of 3.1658386167218754e-5 km (3.17 cm) in position and 1.1143606348524221e-5 km per TDB day in rate across 56,291 boundaries. Their analytic derivatives are piecewise derivatives; there is no exact continuity or global event-time guarantee at those quantized joins. The six-root checks do not establish that a finite-difference search is insensitive to every join. The position-encoding bound alone contributes at most 2.6383e-10 AU/day to the existing 0.001-day finite-difference slope, below the existing 2e-9 AU/day state derivative comparison allowance; evaluator roundoff and physical-source error are separate. This does not establish sixty-second timing near a shallow radial root. Every qualified Saturn root is at least 18,784 seconds from any contributing record join. `native-evidence.json` reproduces these derived distances and the slope bound.

The six source roots were replayed on both representations with the full existing geocentric TT/TDB model. Native Debug and Release roots, source-coefficient comparisons and resource observations are recorded in `native-evidence.json`. `native-captures.json` retains the raw Swift outputs, build receipts, executable size, baseline and toolchain used to create that summary. ## Independent planetary event checks and limits

`../reference-data/capture-planetary-apsides.py` preserves eight native-centered diagnostic windows frozen before publisher retrieval. Each contains 65 Horizons geometric physical-center range-rate samples spanning eight days. Every sampled sign change has a separately archived source refinement to a bracket narrower than one second, including all local crossings rather than only the one nearest the prediction. Exact queries, target/source names, TT semantics, raw responses and hashes are retained. Each primary response has the adjacent `.query.json` recipe required by the reference archive; refinement transcripts retain their per-response queries and hashes. Replay rejects nonfinite epochs and rates. This is independent source data with failure-oriented epoch selection, not an independent orbit-wide event census.

The public API defines closest/farthest orbital distance, and the retained Neptune/Pluto algorithm explicitly seeks the principal orbital extremum despite short-period radial structure. Those definitions already exist; the eight local diagnostic windows do not authorize a new canonicalization rule. Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars and Pluto meet sixty seconds at the sampled local events. The later DE441 foundation adds the owner-approved direct-source complete-cycle selection for Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune: all 4, 6 and 430 physical-center crossings and 2, 2 and 6 system-center crossings are preserved, and the physical crossings corresponding to each source system-center principal pair are tested. Their six signed native residuals range from −9.958 to +9.615 seconds under the unchanged 60-second target. This finite cycle qualification is not a continuous event-time bound.

The Pluto source regression exposed a separate stopping defect in the retained sampled refinement: it stopped when `span / 9 < 60 seconds` and returned `first + span / 18`, near the left edge of the surviving interval. Engine refinement requires the whole span below sixty seconds and returns its midpoint. The frozen Pluto bracket changed from a 194.13-second engine error to 2.85 seconds. The helper serves both Neptune and Pluto; both retain the original discovery and sampling scheme. The former C helper at revisions `96a813af338019471313d03f4331bbf1d316e06e` and `2729e75c3c2d0ad12ad0b062617a69d6bd8007ef` had a 194.22-second error at the same bracket. That historical result does not describe the public API after the #96 cutover.
